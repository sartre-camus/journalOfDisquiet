JOURNAL OF DISQUIET

TUESDAY, JUNE

Melancholy, Boredom

The gloom is thick enough to cut with a dull knife. There's a sickness in the air, a slow rot that everyone ignores. I stepped out to watch the world go by—a parade of ghosts in a grey city. Everyone else has stopped talking, but I'm still spilling words like a broken faucet.

The boredom is a prison sentence I can't seem to finish. Why have I grown so fond of these elaborate laments? Maybe it's the only escape I've got. I'll just continue to do what I do best—complain to the rain.
