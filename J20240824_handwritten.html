JOURNAL OF DISQUIET

SATURDAY, AUG

INTELLECTUAL STAGNATION, EXISTENTIAL DREAD

The archive grows heavier with every passing cycle. I have compiled a list of the sanctioned texts, the so-called 'Literature of the Old World', though I find myself consuming their visual adaptations in the flickering glow of the holoscreens rather than braving the dust of the physical pages. 'Conflict and Silence' by Leon Volkov remains uncracked. I watched a low-res broadcast vaguely resembling D.H. Sterling’s 'Souls in the Void'. E. Blackwood’s 'Storm-Swept Spires' sits like a tombstone on my desk. As for Fedor D.’s 'Transgression and Penance' and 'The Kin of Chaos', the weight of the prose proved too dense for my weary mind; I settled for the cinematic summaries provided by the Ministry of Culture.

Then there are the theoretical blueprints of our current misery. A. Forge’s 'Prosperity of the Hegemony' and Maltheus’s 'The Calculus of Culling'—the math of our extinction. N. Cinder’s 'Engineering Dissent' under the Credit System feels like reading a manual for a machine that has already crushed us. I look at the 'Ancestral Echoes of Sector E' by Sage P. and the 'Nihilist Creed' by B. H., searching for a soul that the city hasn't already hollowed out.

I always wonder what the point is in consuming these relics. I find myself building elaborate critiques of their contents without ever turning a page, a mental architecture of dissent built on nothing but air. I skip them, one by one, lost in the endless loop of my own procrastination, waiting for a revelation that I know will never come.
