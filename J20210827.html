JOURNAL OF DISQUIET

The Recursion Trial - FRIDAY, AUG

Failure, Logic

I finally faced the interrogation for the Serpent's Tongue role at The Iron Syndicate yesterday, after retreating for a week. I answered the riddles posed by The Grand Inquisitor. He pressed me on my contributions to the underground collectives, the scars from previous jobs, and how I stitched them shut. Then came the mechanical trial: write a script to sum the weights in a ledger and find their mean. A simple task, or so it seemed. My mind had grown rusty with the Serpent's Tongue, its syntax slipping through my fingers like smoke. I had to tap into the Global Grid to find the incantations for file manipulation. The Inquisitor demanded I show him my every move through the Spectral Wire, his eyes recording my every hesitation.

The clock was a heartbeat. "Fifteen minutes," he hissed. I scrambled, tearing snippets from The Collective Mind and grafting them into my work. I hit the mark, but the machine wanted a return, not a shout. I pivoted. Then came the abyss: Recursion. A technique for the elegant, a nightmare for the tired. A loop that swallows itself. I was to modify my blunt iterative tool into a recursive beast. I had never sent such a thing into the wild. I had forgotten the base case, the inductive step—the very mathematics of existence. The time bled out. "This guild is for those who speak the tongue fluently," he said, and the wire went dead.

The aftermath was a storm of bile and shame. Four years of study, and I couldn't write a loop that calls itself? I felt like a fraud. I had taught the concept to street urchins once, yet here I was, drowning in it. I retreated into the flickering shadows of the Grid, watching the cynical jests of the Old World philosophers until sleep, fitful and late, finally claimed me.
