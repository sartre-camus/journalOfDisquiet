JOURNAL OF DISQUIET

2021-08-13

Nature, Thoughts

Looked at those plants that I had collected and arranged them in my Safehouse and balcony, realised that they needed care. The aloe plant in the glass bowl had spider web that needed clearing. The glass bowl was meant for having pet fish in it, I did have few goldfishes cycles ago in them, but they died due to lack of oxygen. After that, the fish bowl had been empty and was catching dust. So recently, I thought of using it as a planter. I filled the bowl with brown soil and planted aloe in it. The aloe plant thrived ever since. I took the glass bowl out to the balcony and removed the web. Cleaned the glass bowl with a wet cloth as it had accumulated dust. The aloe plant is green and has white linear patches. It has thorny edges, not as prickly as a cactus. So, I felt the bowl’s volume wasn’t fully utilized. To compensate for the space and also the appearance, I added another small aloe cutting to the soil inside the glass bowl. This cutting was from another pot. Now, the bowl looks filled with the tentacle like aloe stems coming out of the circular opening at the top of the bowl. These tentacle like stems (or leaves, I’m not sure!) of the aloe seem like alien tentacles, something it would spread and engulf whatever comes in its way. It resembles the tentacles of an octopus however, not as flexible or motile as the aquatic animal. The aloe plant, as I read on The Grid, has a good air improving capability. It’s recommended by The High Science Bureau as an indoor plant for air purification as aloe can remove toxic organic chemicals in the air. It also provides oxygen like any other plant. I haven’t been able to sense any direct benefit of the plant though. Nonetheless, it still captivates my eyes in that fish bowl, sitting on top of the book shelf in my Safehouse.

Thoughts

Growing indoor plants can be a hobby as it does reduce some boredom and stress from my life. Looking at the green leaves of these small plants soothes my eyes. Seeing them grow, through tiny stems and slowly unraveling leaves, is something of a spectacle. A slow change but in tiny observable phases. Each new bud fills me with hope. A little hope for the otherwise pessimistic thinking of mine. The two money plants I have on my desk, with their green leaves, cool when touched, with smooth edges, are a thing of beauty. I see shades of light and dark green in their tender stems. The perfect heart shaped leaves are soft to touch. One plant has grown taller than the planter base and maybe needs to be re-potted.
