JOURNAL OF DISQUIET

FRIDAY, FEBRUARY

DISILLUSIONMENT, DECAY, ISOLATION

Yesterday, the city’s perpetual twilight seemed to press heavier. I spent it trailing A. along the industrial periphery of the Grey Mire, the air a metallic tang and the distant rumble of the lower sectors a constant hum. Later, we found ourselves adrift in the artificial glow of The Gilded Cage, an opulent, soulless beacon in the urban sprawl. A dinner of synthetic indulgences, followed by a pointless dance of angles and spheres on the green baize. It was a scandalous hemorrhage of credits for a single, fleeting night, a lavishness that felt like a cheap trick.

The journey back on my battered kinetic-steed was a four-click blur through the rain-slicked canyons, neon bleeding into the grime. Back in my cramped corner of the city, a single, phantom pulse from U. flickered on my comm-link’s display. A missed connection, a potential shadow I wasn't keen to chase. I thumbed a message, a ghosted missive meant for R., through the 'Echo Chamber' network, but the words felt like a tightening noose. Deleted it. The path of aiding R. felt like a descent into a labyrinth of bad choices, a promise of situations I'd sooner forget before they even began. Better to let the current drag them where it may. Better to let the shadows claim their own.

There were other, more immediate ghosts to wrangle anyway. The endless hunt for a steady stream of credits, a sliver of honest pay in this indifferent machine. And the private rituals: devouring forgotten data-scrawls, etching cryptic thoughts onto memory-shards, navigating the illicit data-streams, or losing myself entirely in the cold comfort of simulated labyrinths. Just another day in the great, grey sprawl.
