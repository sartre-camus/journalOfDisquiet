JOURNAL OF DISQUIET

TUESDAY, FEBRUARY

FUTILITY, DREAD, OBLIVION

The pale light of a leaden morning bled into the bruised hues of afternoon, all lost to the relentless assembly of the grav-cycle – a contraption the Shadow had recently acquired, no doubt another futile attempt at outrunning the inevitable. My back, a testament to the city's grim arithmetic, screamed its protest. My hands, calloused by the struggle, felt like petrified claws. Now, as the neon arteries of the city began to throb, painting the rain-streaked window in sickly greens and blues, the Chronos-Gaze on the wall sneered 6:10 PM. I was waiting for the Kid to materialize from the city's churning fog, to drill him on the arcane calculus. A familiar tremor of doubt gnawed at me. Would he even show? This endless vigil, a grim tax on a life already running on borrowed time, a mere fraction of the city’s indifferent sprawl.

To escape the gnawing quiet, I'd tapped into the Aethernet's latest delusion: 'The Lunar Enclave Tapes' on the EchoStream. Another digital opiate to numb the senses, promising weeks of synthetic oblivion. Just yesterday, I'd bled dry the last flickering frame of 'The Grumbles of Old Man X', the complete series a testament to persistent urban misery. My Neuro-Net subscription, my conduit to fleeting digital escapes, was set to sever its connection, today or tomorrow, another thread of the flimsy illusion about to snap. Too many hours lost to the digital frontier of 'Gunslinger's Gambit', a hollow echo of freedom. A grim diversion, yes, but sometimes, a transient balm for the soul. The specter of unfinished business loomed – the crystalline algorithms of the Over-Mind, the coded rituals, the endless digital directives. They weighed heavy, a constant reminder of my own slipping grasp. Time, the city's most merciless currency, was running thin. Would I find enough coin in the shadows to pay my dues?
