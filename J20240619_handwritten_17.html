JOURNAL OF DISQUIET

THE STATIC IN THE WIRE - WEDNESDAY, JUNE

PARANOIA, ISOLATION, DEBT

To The Architect: I see you are requesting a live uplink for the neural-link integration into The Syndicate’s mainframe. I cannot grant this. Real-time holographic transmissions are a trap I no longer walk into. My voice fails me in the glare of the lens—a neural stutter that makes a mockery of conversation. It has been over two cycles since I allowed my image to be captured by the grid. I fear the eyes in the wire; the technology that powers these feeds is a predator of anonymity.

Why perform this theater when the rain-slicked reality of our work happens in the shadows? Encrypted dead-drops and burst-transmissions are the only honest ways to communicate in a city this loud. I remember reaching out through The Cipher last year, seeking a lifeline. You were a ghost then, leaving my pings to rot in the buffer. You saw the distress signals and chose silence. Why should I dance on cue for your meetings now? Just because The Foundation finds my code-weaving useful does not mean I am a disposable cog at your whim.

I have processed your transmission regarding the 'next steps.' It is clear: the credits have dried up. No more payments will hit my ledger for the work I’ve bled into the system. I find I am indifferent. I do not weave these subroutines for the scrap-metal currency of the Low-Rises; I do it to stave off the rot of boredom. It is the only craft I have left in this neon-soaked hell. It is, perhaps, the only thing delaying my inevitable flatline.

On a final note, I suppose I owe a debt of gratitude to The Central Foundation for keeping my terminal powered this long, regardless of the friction. I will continue to forge conduits between these dying systems, hoping to bring some sliver of value to the dregs of the city who still rely on the old protocols. Until the lights go out for good.

BACK TO THE VOID. OR SEEK RANDOM.
