The Biriyani Ritual - SUNDAY, SEPTEMBER

Family, Consumption

Life is a flood of thoughts, a drowning man's inventory. I need a better lexicon. The Silver-Tongued Orator might have a word for it—something long, hollow, and impressive. I envy his way with words, the way he dresses his despair in silk. Should I do the same? My only reader—the man in the mirror—would see through the act.

I ordered the Crimson Feast from the Jade Pavilion. The Courier Guild delivered it just as the light was fading. It’s a substantial meal for the three of us: M, P, and the ghost of myself. I’ve started using their true names, a habit picked up from the old Ramuism broadcasts. It doesn't change the blood between us, but it changes the distance. I address them as fellow travelers in this sinking ship, rather than the roles the city assigned them.
