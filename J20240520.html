JOURNAL OF DISQUIET

MAY 20, 2024

Decay, Existentialism

The machine is stuttering, swallowing letters like a nervous witness. Maybe the wires are frayed, or maybe it's just the rot. I played with the backlight—a neon mask for a dying tool—and it seems to hold for now. I paid a heavy price for this iron and plastic, and watching it crumble feels like watching my own reflection in a cracked mirror.

There's a decadence in the air, a slow-motion collapse of everything I touch. The cells are quitting, the gear is failing, and the 'new' that replaces them tastes like ash. It's all just a long walk through a boring tunnel. Breaking the monotony would cost more than I've got, a tax on the soul I can't pay.

So I seek the big sleep—the closest a man gets to the end without the paperwork. I dream without knowing, a ghost in my own head. What if this whole crawl is just a figment of some cosmic sleeper's nightmare? I might just be the smoke from someone else's cigarette.
