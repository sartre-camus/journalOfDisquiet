JOURNAL OF DISQUIET

THURSDAY, JUN

OBSESSION, NIHILISM

To put things in perspective is a difficult task. I write these as if there's someone interested in what I write, as if suddenly a Monitor is going to peek over my shoulders and check out what is being written. I have considerable doubts whether these words would convey any sort of meaning to them. When my calligraphy skills are at their poorest, will it not be a difficult task to decipher this handwriting of mine to understand what's written? They would give up sooner than anticipated if they started to read whatever I write about.

Somehow this form of writing creates a vacuum in my head—a persistent one, I must admit. It is as if I start running out of thoughts to think and write about. Everything that occurs in my head completely vanishes on this blank paper as it gets transformed into words written with ink. It would take a lot of patience even on my part to read someone's written words. The reason why I struggle reading the old archives is because of the lack of patience. I am desperate to sift through sentences and paragraphs and pages, as if I could finish off a tome within minutes and then be done with it. Unfortunately, that yields nothing but despair followed by unfulfilling thoughts crawling through my head.

I should start looking at those blank pages again to make me think on what it is that I could write—this attempt to write on every blank scrap that I could ever find is something I find a compelling thing to do. I shall find papers that are blank and a pen, then I will start writing as if my life depends on it. The aim is to cover the page with sentences, as long as it might be. But the exercise must not cease until I exhaust all those pages in the mechanical feeder tray. That's where I pick up my sheets from and then start scribbling.

Will writing for longer times cause pain to my body? I'm not sure; of course, the fist and fingers do hurt a bit. The back hurts and so do the shoulders. Writing on a page could be a serious task if it could damage my physical frame. How long till I eventually give up is what I wonder? I must write something else that's not just words, perhaps some mathematical notations or logic forms on paper.

I can barely think of a mathematical problem off-hand that I could write and fill these pages with—how about if I start writing some mainframe logic-chains instead? Like some code snippets using some programming languages that I had learnt in the Academy. Some Core-C sprinkled with some Net-Script or J-Synth or another language; even pseudo-code of some form should do. That'll perhaps improve my terminal fluency a tad bit.

There should be no attempt made to write the above, because I do not wish to improve at anything—I have no desire to excel in the 'craft' that I undertake. I would like to underperform, misdeliver, and skimp off on the Bureau mandates.

BACK TO THE VOID. OR SEEK RANDOM.
