JOURNAL OF DISQUIET

2022-01-11

STRUGGLE, ILLUSION, ASCENSION

Just yesterday, the flickering ghost of a memory played across my retinal display: a cracked optical reel featuring the enigmatic Dr. Silas Veridian, peddling his latest opiate for the masses, 'The Grand Delusion,' itself a cold recitation of the grim assertions of Old Man K. Its spectral narration spoke of the gnawing teeth of hardship, the relentless grind of failure, and the hollow defiance required to claw oneself above the muck.

That projected sermonette showed Veridian scaling the jagged, snow-choked heights of the Atlas Spine, far beyond the choked skylines of the city. He clawed his way to the summit, gasping for the thin air, supposedly to find a desolate panorama – a vista stark and unforgiving, that would steal breath, if there were any left to steal. And then, the grim pronouncement: it’s only when a soul endures, grinds its teeth through the pervasive suffering, the bottomless pit of misery, to glimpse a wider, colder truth, to see the vast, indifferent machinery of it all… only then, perhaps, a fleeting, phantom satisfaction, a whisper of accomplishment, a hollow echo of what some still call 'happiness' might settle. A lie, perhaps, but a comforting one, for a moment, before the rain begins again, washing away the fleeting illusion, and plunging us back into the familiar gloom of the district below.

BACK TO THE VOID. OR SEEK RANDOM.
