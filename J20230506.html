JOURNAL OF DISQUIET

SATURDAY, MAY

LUXURY, DEBT

Spending my twilight hours at the Embassy Row Hub—or more often, The Grand Continental, where I haunt the lobby for no good reason. Some of the staff are starting to recognize the face of a regular. Does that buy me a seat at the high table? Not a chance. It’s just another excuse to exist. Why can’t I just stay in the shadows of the Hub? This obsession with the Continental, splurging borrowed cash on a life I haven't earned—it’s a dangerous game. Without a steady score, I can’t keep this independent front moving. I’m trapped in a loop of banal and futile events. For some reason, the neon world outside is more real to me than the one inside my own skull. I chase the external, observing the rudimentary decay of the streets while I obsess over the serenity of luxury I can't afford. Why don't these other drifters just shut up with their loser talk and pointless gossip?

How long can I keep up this charade at The Grand Continental? Eventually, they’ll see me for what I am—a man with no business there, just taking up space. They’ll blacklist me and throw me into the street soon enough. I’ve wasted time and borrowed money that should have been used to buy my way out of this town. What keeps me going? What makes me think I’ll always land on my feet when the collectors come knocking? This belief that everything will be fine, despite the mounting debt, is the biggest lie of all. It’s going to collapse, and I’ll have nothing left but these entries and the fading memories of expensive scents and spa treatments. Just memories, and the cold reality of the gutter.
