JOURNAL OF DISQUIET

THE MATTER-PULSE PROTOCOL - MONDAY, AUG

OBSOLESCENCE, ISOLATION, DECAY

There is a particular brand of tragedy in finding the will to labor during the grid-down cycles of the weekend, only to spend the standard shifts sulking in the neon-drenched gloom of my hab-unit. Today, the artificial dawn of a new cycle breaks over the Iron Docks, and I find myself strangely eager to interface with the machine—whatever 'work' means in this sprawling, rain-slicked graveyard of industry.

I scramble through the corrupted archives of my previous assignments, hunting for some easy scrap or a low-hanging data-leak to sustain myself. But the realization hits like a cold rain: every bridge to my old contacts in the Syndicate has been burned. Those connections are dead, buried under layers of encrypted silence, and no amount of digital necromancy can resurrect them.

I’ve been obsessing over a plan to reboot the 'Matter-Pulse' protocol. It’s a simple task, or so I tell myself: just a few patches to the code, some automated diagnostic subroutines to catch the fractures in the system before it collapses again. A minor fix for a world that’s already broken beyond repair.

I recently stumbled upon a fragment from 'The Codex of Despair' by The Melancholy Clerk while drifting through the data-streams of the Great Nihilism. It spoke to the very rot I feel in my bones, a perfect mirror to this existence of high-tech decay.

BACK TO THE VOID. OR SEEK RANDOM.
