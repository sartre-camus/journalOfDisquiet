SOLITUDE, HEAT

My primary obsession has become these dispatches. I spend an ungodly amount of time documenting the slow rot of my own mind. Is this the path forward? Just recording every stray thought and every hour I waste in this concrete maze? It’s been a stagnant day, drifting through The Neon District and haunting the hotel lobbies with no purpose other than to exist. I was hunting for a familiar face in the sea of strangers, yet the idea of actually speaking to someone terrifies me. What could I possibly say that would matter? I’d rather remain a ghost, watching the world through a haze of smoke. I’m too tired to play the part of a social creature. approaching someone and saying "Hi" feels like a surrender. Yesterday, I made a fool of myself. I tried to strike up a conversation with The Silver Sage—a man with a beard like a winter storm sitting opposite me. I thought he was an outsider, a traveler from a distant shore. Turns out he was just a local drifter struggling with the house network. I tried to play the technician, but the machine was dead to my touch. I had to abandon him, mumbling some excuse about leaving him to his "work." It’s pathetic how far I’ll go to find someone who needs a savior, only to fail them. Why do I bother? I can't keep chasing other people's shadows. I want to crawl back to my room and disappear, but the heat in that box is a living thing. I can't even turn on the machine without feeling like I’m in a furnace. And the voices—the thin walls are a curse. I’d rather spend my days in the lobbies of The Collective Hive or The Grand Marquis than return to that dust-choked oven. There’s no sanctuary here; I can hear every word of the illicit deal being discussed in the next booth. The heat is rising, and without the rain to wash this city clean, sleep is a distant memory. The fan just pushes the hot air around, choking me. I’m so damn tired. I thought about taking a room at The Transient's Rest for a few nights, but it’s a sucker’s bet—too expensive and cursed. The last time I stayed there, I spent two weeks shivering in a fever. I haven't forgotten the price I paid.
