JOURNAL OF DISQUIET

The Confessional Debrief - SUNDAY, JANUARY

introspective, solitude

I've been replaying the words from the session with the soul-doctor. 'How does the sting of a dame's rejection settle in your bones?', 'Why does a simple "No" feel like a slug to the chest?' Maybe it's the pride—a high-stakes game of self-regard that teeters on the edge of narcissism. The dating racket? I haven't cracked the code. My past flings are ghosts that taught me nothing. Every new dame is a fresh case, a new labyrinth. I can't change the hardware; I am what I am. That's why finding a partner in this neon-lit void is taking longer than a cold winter. I blame the Syndicate of Morality we live under—a town built on rigid rules and hollow rituals. It's designed for the 'socially acceptable,' a club I was never meant to join. I had dreams of skipping this city for the Old World—Western Europe—where a man's choices are his own. I tried for the Cold North, the Lowlands, the Baltic reaches. Interviews came and went, but the grit wasn't there to close the deal. I'm anchored by the Matriarch's comforts, mollycoddled until the fire went out. This hesitation to bleed for the dream is the root of my despair. The grass is always greener in the West, but the lure of the easy life here is a siren song that keeps me lazy, keeps me procrastinating, keeps me trapped in the sprawl.
