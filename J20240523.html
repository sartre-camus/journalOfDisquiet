JOURNAL OF DISQUIET

THURSDAY, MAY

Poverty, Inertia

I'm slumped in the shadows of a rented room, flat broke and out of luck. The debts are stacking up—not just the green kind, but the heavy weight of favors and unkept promises. This city doesn't give anything for free.

Even the machines I rely on start to turn cold the moment I stop feeding them my time. In this racket, if you aren't moving, you're rusting.
