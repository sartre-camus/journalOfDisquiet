JOURNAL OF DISQUIET

MONDAY, MAY

PARANOIA, ISOLATION

I finally took the long trek to The Hive-Node Sector 7, attempting to purge the phobia of those desolate streets. I’ve been in a coded exchange with Overseer Vaelin regarding the Aether-Grid integration progress. He demands more progress but offers no credits. My invoice remains unpaid, yet he requests a visual uplink for a 'personal demonstration' of the work completed. I hesitate. If the invite hits my terminal, I may simply ignore it.

He has no intent to settle his debts despite the logs. Refusal is the only weapon left. Engaging further only leads to circular arguments about trust and technical capability—concepts he barely grasps. I will not be interrogated over an uplink, regardless of the credits. It is better to let the matter rot and focus on the architecture itself. I will continue to tinker with the integration for my own ends, regardless of the Overseer’s whims.

My tactical plan from here: Modify the Neural-Link and Aether-Grid protocols so that outbound data-streams can be handled. Integrate the latest upstream shifts into the local build. Perhaps I will finally patch the Core-Protocol branch with my own custom modifications.

My scalp burns from the toxic dust and smog of the transit. The stagnant runoff in the gutters is a breeding ground for parasites. I fear the Grey-Rot or Sector-Fever. On the return journey, I must stick to the starboard side of the thoroughfare to watch the oncoming hover-traffic and avoid the sludge. I left my optics behind on purpose—too much glare from the artificial suns. I must be cautious. I managed to scavenge two stim-packets for the walk back.

I intend to digitize this parchment and transmit it to my private server so I don't have to carry physical evidence through the checkpoints. It’s a cleaner method; I can utilize the station’s tools and then vanish the hard copy. Perhaps I’ll even send a handwritten threat to Vaelin using this very technique.

BACK TO THE VOID. OR SEEK RANDOM.
