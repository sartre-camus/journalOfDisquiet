DECEPTION, STAGNATION, DEBT

The rain was a steady drumbeat against the rusted shell of the Sector 9 relay station. I stood there, feeding credits into a dying terminal, weaving a web of lies for the Nexus Syndicate. I spoke of protocols I’d never breached and systems I’d never seen, as if I actually believed the fiction myself. I remember the Handler’s voice crackling through the static, feeding me prompts that I turned into pure garbage. It was a masterpiece of deception, reframing my digital dossier to fit their cold, corporate requirements.

Now, the Syndicate is demanding proficiency in Neural-Scripting and Binary-Flux. It’s been cycles since I touched those ancient dialects. I’ll have to scrub my identity patch again, rewrite the cover-logs, and then wait for a signal that might never come. The Great Shutdown is approaching, which means the city’s gears will grind to a halt. I might not hear a whisper until the next solar cycle.

I’m drowning in my own inaction, reciting old adages like a broken machine to justify the stagnation. I find myself searching for patterns in the static—some kind of confirmation bias to explain why I’m still here, rotting in the shadows. I don't even know why I used that term.
