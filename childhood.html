Memories of childhood

From games to studies

I loved playing cricket, video games and computers since I was five years old. I never used to miss a single cricket match on television. I watched every ball of a test match game, collected trivia, cricket game cards and played them with my friends. I had lot of friends. I also had loved gaming since the age of ten or even earlier. I had seen the TV video game back in 1995 at one of my cousin’s place and enjoyed watching others play. Sometimes, I got to play too. These were the times when I cared very little about studies. I was an average kid when it came to studies. I was bad at Maths too. I remember getting a ’D’ grade in one of the tests when I was in fourth standard. I didn’t care to show the report card to my father. I had to forge his signature on the report card and give it back to my teacher. My father would have caned me if he got to know that I had gotten bad grades in a test. Sometimes, I used to ask my mother to sign, but she would refuse and ask me to go to my father instead. I didn’t worry much about it. All I wanted was to play and enjoy the time. I spent evenings playing cricket in my neighbourhood. Most of the holidays involved playing board games like chess, carrom or cricket outside. I was so much interested in cricket that I wanted my father to put me through cricket coaching. He refused. I watched one of my friend who played well, he went to coaching and later represented the State in cricket. I would spend a lot of time watching cartoons on TV. I remember waking up as early as 5.00 AM to watch one. It was the time at which the cartoon channel started its broadcast. I enjoyed playing video games so much that I would invite my friends over sometime or go to their place and play. I spent hours trying to beat the game. One day, my father got transferred and we had to move to new city. A city with no friends. All I had left was home and school and nothing in between. It was during this time that my father started taking interest in my studies. He wanted me to study well. I was supposed to be more disciplined with school work. He used to sit in front of me with a stick to ensure that I studied and didn’t wander away. I was beaten and trained. My father used to yell at me and say to my mother not to feed me unless I did my work. I had no choice but to study. I had to be well behaved, get good grades, and I wouldn’t have any trouble with my father. I even remember my father mentioning to one of his friends over phone as to how he trained me to study.

A missed opportunity

It was early morning when I reached school just like any other day. I had the habit of reaching early, never late. My classmate Naveen was early too and he wanted to talk to me privately about some matter. So we went for a little walk along the corridors of the school. He said that there’s a girl who has a crush on me and he would disclose her name only if I’m interested in pursuing her. I was surprised and amused. Girl having a crush on me? A studious boy who’s scared of talking to girls and only cared about better grades and not running into conflicts with teachers or school management. A risk averse wallflower like me wouldn’t possibly think that someone would really have a crush on me. I was eager to know who it was and asked him. He was adamant, he said only if I was willing to go after her, that he would tell me her name. I said I can’t go after a girl as I would be distracted from the studies and would affect my grades. I found that going on a date or pursuing a love relationship at my age of fifteen was too risky. I said, “I can’t pursue her, I’ve got to study and do well in the exams”. At that time, I was too shy and conservative in my demeanour. I was pursuing a goal of being a topper in the school. I wanted my name to be etched on the plaque containing the list of all the students who scored the highest marks every year. This year it had to be me. Naveen was taken aback. He said this is a chance for you at love and you’re rejecting it for your studies. If I were you, I would have pursued her till the end. I didn’t know what to say. I begged him to let me be and get back to class as it was getting late. After few months, as things passed by and I had forgotten about this conversation, Naveen told me her name. She was beautiful and sexy. I remember my other classmates used to stare her during a boring lecture or otherwise. I remember I had shared my class notes with her in the past and she was happy for my help. Once, she was too informal when she called me, she addressed me as ’Da..’ or something similar, the other girls giggled and made fun of her, I didn’t pay much attention and I took my notes back from her. But I never expected that she’d have a crush on me - an average looking nerd. I was disappointed. I wish I at least spoke to her, had I known that she liked me. It was too late and she had moved on, probably busy preparing for the upcoming final exam. I remember glancing at her when she was writing the exam, I was done with my answers and just looked back at her. She was busy writing, bit worried, and anxious, as the exam duration was coming to end. I felt sorry for her. Had I ’pursued her’ as my classmate said, I would have been more closer to her, maybe helped her in doing better in her exams. If not love, at least I’d have a friend. Back then, these didn’t worry me much. I didn’t care about what I missed, as long as I was doing well with my studies. She left the school after that year and went somewhere else. And that’s the last of what I’ve seen of her - in the exam hall. Facebook wasn’t there back then or it was probably the age of Orkut. I didn’t have a computer let alone internet to get in touch with her. I moved on with my usual business only till after about six years I befriended her on Facebook. She’d moved to another country. I was going through her posts and found that she was watching a TV series and was looking for some episodes online. I took this opportunity to find links of streaming websites for that TV series and sent her a message. She thanked me, but it wasn’t what she was looking for. I gave up. That’s the end of it. I realised it’s no point for me to catch up on lost love opportunities after years and expect to build a relationship. And by the way the girl’s name was Neha. She was really cute and funny. I’m also reminded by what one of my classmates Dhiraj, who was very good in studies, better than me, told me back in school, “By being shy and not talking to girls and keeping away from them what are you achieving? Aren’t they just human as you are? Why do you run away from them? As if they’d make your life a failure. You don’t know what you’re missing. When you’re old, you’d recollect this and realise.” Also, I never topped the class, my name was never etched on the plaque.

We decide and you do

I had finished my tenth standard exams and I had the choice of studying either science, commerce or arts for the next stage of my education. I was in conversation with my cousin, who was working in the software industry back then, and wanted to know what I liked studying the most. I did mention to him that I like Maths. Especially, topics related arithmetic involving calculation of taxes, profit, loss, interest etc. So he suggested that I could take up commerce and study more about business and finance. He also suggested that there is a bright future for Chartered Accountancy. I was convinced by his opinion and conveyed the same to my father. I said I wanted to study commerce. But the response I got from my father was something that I hadn’t expected. At first he rejected my idea and said it’s not worthwhile to study commerce. Later, he just stopped speaking to me. He started giving me the silent treatment and started snubbing me. I was pretty close with my father back then. I used to talk about my successes, problems, failures and sought his guidance in general. Seeing him go cold turkey on me made me feel ostracised. I felt as if I had said something forbidden. Then, I mentioned about this incident back to my cousin. He spoke with my father and asked him not to do what he’s doing. I also remember my father trying to influence my decision of studying commerce by asking his friend and neighbour to talk to me and convince me to study science. The neighbour said it’s not of any use studying commerce. I was made to reconsider my decision of what I’d study based on the marks I had scored in my tenth exams. I was told that I had good marks and I should be studying science. I for one didn’t know much back then. I did like maths and studying science also involved maths as one of the subjects in its course. However, the maths in commerce had to more with accounts and business. In science it had to do more with calculus, trigonometry, geometry etc, the topics that I wasn’t comfortable studying. Finally, I decided to study science, because there was some kind of stigma associated with studying anything else apart from science. Arts didn’t even stand a chance worth mentioning about!

Trouble with studying

I was in my twelfth standard and was studying Physics. I had trouble understanding the concepts and solving problems. I found it to be very difficult. Chemistry wasn’t easy on me either. I had to remember so many formulae, equations, concepts and so on that I had to work extra hard for the marks I scored in the tests. I never sought any coaching or tutoring help from anybody. I was all alone. I had to read the text books, understand and make notes, then, practice them well before I gave the tests and exams. If I failed to understand a certain concept, I had no choice but to memorize for the sake of scoring marks in the exam. This happened to me in one of the tests, it was a Physics exam and I had hadn’t studied well for it. I was so scared that I didn’t want to fail it. I started troubling my father saying that I can’t go to the test. I can’t go and write. I’ll give an excuse of falling sick and skip it. I was worried that failing in that test could make me attend extra classes conducted by the Physics teacher, whom I was even more scared of. I did not go to the test and skipped it saying I was sick and asked for a re-test. This was not the only episode of me trying to escape the wrath of failure in exams. Somewhere, during the mid year, I started getting anxious about this whole exam situation and also the monthly tests that I had to appear for. I one day decided that I would drop out of the course and would study something else. I started pleading the same to my father. I wanted to drop out and study commerce. Then one of my relatives stepped in and tried talking to me. He said dropping out now will make me lose a year and it’s not worth it. Just try with whatever you’ve got, study and try to get through. I was also taken to a child psychologist at a mental health hospital in my city to seek counseling and address my fear and anxiety. I was worried about my condition and also why am I being taken to a mental institution for help. My father had also spoken with the Physics teacher about me and my condition. The teacher was shocked to hear that and asked me why I was skipping tests, these tests won’t count for the final score and what’s the reason behind worrying so much about it. She said to me that my success in life in general wouldn’t depend on how much marks I scored in her subject and there are plenty of tests, exams that I would have to face in my education span. My father said to the teacher that I needed counseling and would take me to a counselor soon. My first interaction with a mental health professional was when I was seventeen years old. I didn’t feel comfortable talking about it and just said that I had trouble with studying. The counseling didn’t help me much. It was just one session. The counselor spoke with me and my father separately. This anxiety about studies had made me contemplate suicide at one point. Although, I didn’t think about it any further or attempt one such. I felt the pressure of performing well from my peers and parents. That was the reason behind my anxiety and fear. I was given a lot of advice by relatives and cousin. They taught me how to meditate and keep myself calm. I meditated every day for half hour and that helped me. It made less anxious and somehow focus better. I was able to regain my ability to study and did well to pass the final exam. However, the exam anxiety didn’t end there, it held on to me for another year and I faced episodes of it just before the test. I was supported by my cousin, who used to talk to me and listen to me. This somehow helped later and was I able to cope with it.

First ’girl’ acquaintance

It was when I was fifteen years old, that this happened. I had done well in an exam and my father was happy about it. He took me to his friend’s place nearby and introduced me. My father’s friend wanted me to meet his daughter Ananya, she was studying in the same class as mine in another school and was good with studies too. Ananya, was an average looking girl wearing specs with dark complexion and curly hair. She smiled at me and said, ’Hello’. Then, she wanted help with some books and study material, which I promised to give her. My father and his friend were besides us and they went on with their chat and reunion. Ananya was someone who worked very hard and studied everything she had to. I remember her studying every line in a text book and making sure she knew it well. She shared some of the maths reference text books she used. I found it useful, I also noted it had some hand drawn smileys and gestures in it. Probably, it was something to keep her motivated and not feel jinxed about doing Math. I even remember discussing some of the questions she had given to me with my teacher in class. Those weren’t the questions that one would easily find in the text book. The teacher felt happy that someone is taking interest in the subject and trying to ask questions about it. Ananya and I used to meet every now and then before tests and exchange books and notes. She once came to my home with her mother on an evening. I was somewhere else. She entered my room and opened the shelves and started exploring the books I had. Later, I came and helped her with what she needed. She was eager to get what she came for. On another occasion before the exam, I remember going to her place with my mother to discuss few topics that could appear in the question paper in the upcoming exam. I entered her room and found her studying. I also noticed pencil sketches on the wall. They were well drawn. She asked me whether something in the text would be asked for exam or not. I advised her not to worry so much and learn what’s important. Then, we gave the exams and it all ended. The results were out. She had scored slightly more than what I had. I didn’t care to talk her after that as I got involved in some other things. It was holidays, the exams were done, I wanted to play. I tried hard to persuade my father to buy me this hand-held gaming console as I had done well in the exams. He agreed and I bought one. I started playing the new games in 3D on hand held device laying on my bed and forgot about the world. Later, one day I received a call from Ananya, she mentioned that she was going to join a coaching center for getting entry to few best colleges. She also expected that I would have called her before and enroll for coaching. It was too late for me and I didn’t care much about coaching. I thought I can study on my own and needed no help. She tried to tell me that in order to get through the entrance exams for these colleges, I would need coaching and also recommended another coaching center which I could still join. I didn’t pay much heed to it and continued with my life. We stopped talking to each other after that. After two years, when I had finished my twelfth standard, we met again. I was out for a walk with my mother and I saw Ananya riding her bicycle and her mother was there too. My mother and hers started talking about college admissions and others. Ananya continued pedalling her bicycle around and later when we were about to leave, wished good luck with my future prospects. She said nothing more than that. I felt it was condescending. For all the help we did to each other two years ago was forgotten, she was now fixated on her college prospects and didn’t care to ask what I had been doing. I felt snubbed. Also realised that there wasn’t anything between us to talk about. I too forgot about this incident and joined college where I spent four years studying and trying to learn skills needed for my career. It was not until I started working in my first job that I was reminded on Ananya, my father one day said he had met Ananya’s father at some place and got to know that she had done really well in college and had a very high paying job at a private company. I was working in a public sector company back then, and felt ashamed of what I had done with my career. I tried to know about her through Facebook, and even sent a friend request. She had over a thousand of them and didn’t care to accept mine. I was a nobody to her. She later went on do PhD in an Ivy League college abroad and has been very successful in her career from what I could gather. I for one didn’t care much to make something of myself. Sometimes, I wonder if I had listened to her about what she said to me over the phone years ago about enrolling into coaching and getting through an entrance exam for the best colleges. If I had, I might have done well or got to meet her more often. I envy her success. Makes me wonder why I am not so successful as her. From what I remember we had similar acumen and intelligence, at least at the age of fifteen. It’s because of my choices. The choices I made versus hers. Well, she was my first girl acquaintance and even considered her my friend back when we used to talk. However, that didn’t last too long. Did I love her secretly? Maybe, I can’t say. But I did feel attracted to her. I am sure she never felt the same about me.
