JOURNAL OF DISQUIET

J20230504 - THURSDAY, MAY

OBSERVATION, FUTILITY

The decrypted entries of these journals. Unlike the handwritten scribbles on napkins, these typed characters feel cold, sharp, and far more palatable to the senses. The legibility is a trap. The wait stretches on for the runners to bring word from the syndicates and fronts I’ve petitioned for work. How can I claim to be a free agent when I’m still waiting for a payout from others? What is there left for me to do? The open-source racket I started has hit a wall; I haven't the slightest idea how to turn code into cold, hard cash. The idea of begging for handouts to support my 'work' makes my skin crawl. I remember a woman from the first of May—a ghost in a kurta and salwar, looking like she stepped out of a different era or a different continent. They say she was a civil engineer, but in this city, everyone’s got a cover. I remember the gray at her temples, her hair pulled back like she had nothing to hide and everything to lose. She wore the same muted colors day after day, a stoic mask that never slipped. She only spoke when the situation demanded a silver bullet. Otherwise, she was a statue of silence, the fatigue of the city etched into her brow. She looked tired, like she’d been carrying the weight of the whole damn district on her shoulders.

The obsession with doing nothing. Is all this effort even what I want? I choose the void, yet I expect the world to keep spinning for me. I’ve become utterly unemployable, a ghost in the machine. No one can get a day’s work out of me now. My existence is a flickering neon sign in a deserted alley—futile and fading. I realize with every shallow breath that the end is coming—the agony, the failures, the long, dark walk into despondence.

BACK TO THE VOID. OR SEEK RANDOM.
