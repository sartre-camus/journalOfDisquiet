JOURNAL OF DISQUIET

MAY 18, 2024

Debt, Regret

After the rain-soaked drain of the last forty-eight, I'm back at the desk. I've squared the ledger with 'The Ghost', at least for tonight. But 'The Creditor' still holds my markers. He told me to stack enough bills to choke a horse before I come knocking. In this city, savings are just a polite word for a head start I'll never have.

I slipped back into the neon-lit gutters of my past last Thursday, the old habits fitting like a cheap suit. Suddenly, the ego flared—a flash-in-the-pan sense of owning the sidewalk. I haven't learned to live with the man in the mirror yet, haven't endured the itch to over-think and over-write. I'm a traitor to my own vows of simplicity, just another slave to the senses.

I need to learn to carry the weight of my own shadow—the boredom, the apathy, the entitlement. Maybe I'll switch to ink and pulp. No juice, no trail. Just the scratch of a nib against a blank page, leaving a smaller footprint in this concrete jungle.
